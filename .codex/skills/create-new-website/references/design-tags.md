# Design Tags

Use this reference only when choosing or translating an art direction. Combine tags into a coherent system; do not treat the list as a checklist.

## Build a Direction

Choose one dominant tag from each category that materially affects the design, then add two or three supporting tags. Keep tensions intentional, such as `editorial + technical` or `minimal + playful`. Avoid combinations whose visual rules conflict without a clear hierarchy.

For every proposed direction, translate the tags into decisions for:

- typography: family character, scale, weight, rhythm, and alignment;
- palette: temperature, contrast, accent behavior, and surface hierarchy;
- composition: grid, density, whitespace, overlap, and hero model;
- geometry: corners, borders, shadows, containers, and dividers;
- imagery: photography, illustration, diagrams, icons, or product UI;
- motion: pace, amplitude, triggers, and reduced-motion behavior.

## Mood and Voice

- `authoritative`: stable hierarchy, measured spacing, restrained accents
- `approachable`: warm copy, rounded geometry, generous rhythm
- `bold`: large type, decisive contrast, compressed choices
- `calm`: low visual noise, soft contrast, slow motion
- `editorial`: typographic hierarchy, asymmetric pacing, image-led storytelling
- `expressive`: unexpected scale, color, cropping, or composition
- `luxury`: sparse layouts, precise type, rich materials, quiet interaction
- `playful`: bright accents, varied shapes, lively microinteractions
- `technical`: grids, diagrams, data cues, compact labels
- `utilitarian`: direct hierarchy, dense information, minimal decoration

## Composition and Hero

- `centered-statement`: one dominant message with focused action
- `split-hero`: copy and image or product view share the first screen
- `editorial-crop`: oversized image or type extends beyond the frame
- `product-stage`: interface or object is the main visual proof
- `poster-like`: flat composition, dramatic type, deliberate asymmetry
- `modular-grid`: repeated cells establish rhythm and hierarchy
- `bento`: varied card sizes organize several related value points
- `narrative-scroll`: sections reveal a sequential argument
- `dense-dashboard`: compact controls, data, and operational context
- `quiet-whitespace`: few elements with large spatial separation

## Typography

- `grotesk`: neutral or contemporary sans-serif structure
- `humanist`: open, readable, conversational sans-serif character
- `geometric`: constructed forms and crisp repetition
- `serif-led`: editorial, literary, or premium emphasis
- `monospace-accent`: technical labels, metadata, or code-like detail
- `oversized-type`: typography becomes the primary visual device
- `compact-type`: high information density and efficient scanning
- `high-contrast-scale`: dramatic jumps between display and body sizes
- `restrained-scale`: close, disciplined levels for calm or utility
- `mixed-case-display`: expressive headings with controlled body copy

## Color and Contrast

- `monochrome`: hierarchy comes from value, type, and spacing
- `warm-neutral`: cream, sand, clay, or brown-biased surfaces
- `cool-neutral`: white, gray, slate, or blue-biased surfaces
- `dark`: dark dominant canvas with carefully managed contrast
- `high-contrast`: sharp text, border, and surface separation
- `soft-contrast`: tonal layering with minimal hard edges
- `single-accent`: one color carries actions and emphasis
- `bold-color`: saturated fields or multiple strong color regions
- `duotone`: two dominant hues control the entire system
- `cultural-palette`: locally meaningful colors used with explicit rationale

## Surfaces and Geometry

- `flat`: hierarchy without shadows or simulated depth
- `bordered`: rules and outlines organize content
- `card-based`: contained surfaces group related information
- `layered`: overlapping planes create depth and focus
- `glass`: translucent surfaces used sparingly over legible backgrounds
- `sharp`: square or nearly square corners and precise alignment
- `rounded`: soft corners and friendly controls
- `pill-controls`: highly rounded actions, filters, or labels
- `soft-shadow`: subtle elevation and separation
- `hard-shadow`: graphic, offset depth with poster or playful character

## Imagery and UI Language

- `photographic`: real people, places, products, or processes
- `art-directed-photo`: deliberate crops, lighting, and color treatment
- `illustrative`: custom drawings or conceptual scenes
- `collage`: layered cutouts, textures, and mixed media
- `diagrammatic`: flows, systems, maps, or explanatory graphics
- `product-ui`: authentic interface views demonstrate value
- `data-led`: charts, numbers, and evidence shape the composition
- `icon-led`: recognizable symbols support scanning
- `typographic`: type and layout carry the visual identity without imagery
- `ambient-graphic`: gradients, glows, fields, or textures support hierarchy

## Motion and Interaction

- `static`: rely on composition and state changes only
- `subtle-motion`: short fades, shifts, and hover feedback
- `ambient-motion`: slow background movement without blocking reading
- `scroll-reveal`: content enters as the narrative progresses
- `kinetic-type`: restrained typographic movement as a signature device
- `playful-motion`: bounces, elastic easing, or surprising hover states
- `product-motion`: demonstrate workflows or interface transitions
- `cinematic`: staged, slower sequences with strong visual continuity

Always preserve usability: support reduced motion, keep controls predictable, and avoid animation that delays access to content.

## Density and Conversion

- `minimal`: few messages and actions per viewport
- `balanced`: concise narrative with selective supporting proof
- `content-rich`: detailed explanations with strong sectional hierarchy
- `compact`: reduced spacing and high scan efficiency
- `conversion-led`: repeated clear calls to action and proof near decisions
- `trust-led`: credentials, evidence, process, and reassurance dominate
- `exploratory`: multiple paths invite browsing rather than one funnel
- `application-like`: persistent controls and task-oriented navigation

## Cultural and Local Identity

- `locally-rooted`: use place, language, materials, or visual history with specificity
- `vernacular`: adapt familiar local signage, print, or interface conventions
- `craft`: emphasize material texture, process, and human production
- `institutional`: civic, academic, archival, or public-service cues
- `international`: neutral system optimized for multilingual reach

Do not reduce cultural identity to flags, stereotypes, or decorative motifs. Tie it to the project’s audience, content, typography support, and real context.

## Translate Tags into a Profile

Record the chosen direction in this compact form:

```text
Design profile
- Core direction: <memorable name>
- Tags: <6–10 compatible tags>
- Signature device: <one recognizable visual behavior>
- Avoid: <2–4 anti-tags or patterns>
- Reference relationship: <what to preserve, reinterpret, or reject>
```

Use the profile as a test during implementation. At least three major decisions should visibly express it, and the result should not drift into the listed anti-tags.
